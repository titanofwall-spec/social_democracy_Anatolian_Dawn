# Cyprus field-report events and July 30 ending

The seven new events each have two choices, hidden military requirements and four roll outcomes. Combined events appear on 28 July, 30 July and 13 August. The first four extend Atilla I to nine decisions (720 maximum); the final three have a separate ceasefire-period score (240 maximum). Historical requirements remain Outdated; alternative requirements are Adequate. Support costs and the +6 next-roll cap are unchanged, and support remains available through 13 August.

The July 30 report precedes the ending. New campaigns receive their calculated leverage reward when that ending is displayed; previously awarded legacy rewards are not awarded twice. The ending represents the game chapter closing with Geneva I, not a claim that the historical first offensive continued uninterrupted until July 30.

Reports distinguish confirmed occupation, movement towards a place and consolidation. Report dates are not silently treated as exact capture dates. Later reports acknowledge continued fighting after the ceasefire.

## 25 July 1974 — Geneva opens amid continued fighting

Scheduled: 25 July 1974.

Source references: S/11353/Add.7, paragraphs 1–5 (25 July; describes developments on 24 July); S/11568, paragraph 7 (Geneva I, 25–30 July)

Geneva I opens with Turkey, Greece and Britain negotiating the ceasefire and the island's future. The airport remains under UN control, with Turkish troops to its north and National Guard troops to its south.

The latest UN account describes gains made during 24 July around northern Lefkoşa, including the Omorphita and Trakhonas areas, Hermes Street and positions near the British High Commission. These are local changes within the city, not the capture of British military bases. West of Lefkoşa, troops have reached the vicinity of Alayköy/Gerolakkos; this report does not yet establish occupation of the village.



## 26 July 1974 — The enclave expands

Scheduled: 26 July 1974.

Source references: S/11353/Add.8, paragraphs 1–4 (25–26 July); S/11353/Add.9, paragraph 1 (26 July, 23:00)

Reports covering the evening of 25 July and the morning of 26 July confirm Alayköy/Gerolakkos under Turkish occupation. Most of the remaining salient in the Trakhonas–Omorphita area of northern Lefkoşa has also been occupied.

Tanks and troops move south along the Girne road and then east towards Değirmenlik/Kythrea. Other troops reach the vicinity of Çamlıbel/Myrtou, while parts of the road towards Alayköy come under Turkish control. These movements do not establish the capture of Çamlıbel or Değirmenlik. The ceasefire holds more steadily elsewhere on the island.



## 27–28 July 1974 — Fighting on the flanks

Scheduled: 28 July 1974.

Source references: S/11353/Add.10, paragraphs 1–3 (27–28 July)

Turkish positions are consolidated around Photta, while Ayios Ermolaos/Şirinevler is reported Turkish-held. Buffavento Castle has also been occupied. The reports confirm these positions by 28 July without identifying an exact capture time for each.

Heavy fighting begins around Koutsoventis on the evening of 27 July, but the village remains in National Guard hands on the afternoon of 28 July. Fighting then develops along the coast near Ayios Epiktitos/Çatalköy. Alsancak/Karavas, Lapta/Lapithos and Çamlıbel/Myrtou remain outside Turkish control in this account.



## 29–30 July 1974 — Geneva I concludes

Scheduled: 30 July 1974.

Source references: S/11353/Add.12, paragraphs 1–2 (29–31 July); S/11398, Geneva Declaration (30 July); A/10002, paragraphs 67–68 (Geneva Declaration)

Fighting continues near Ayios Epiktitos/Çatalköy on 29 July. On 30 July, the UN reports only sporadic shooting around the edges of the main enclave; it does not identify a substantial new advance for that day.

Geneva I concludes with a declaration requiring the areas controlled by opposing armed forces not to be extended. The ministers agree on a UN-supervised security zone around the Turkish-held area and the evacuation of Greek or Greek Cypriot forces from Turkish Cypriot enclaves, which are to receive UN protection. The declaration records the existence in practice of two autonomous administrations, while leaving the wider constitutional settlement for further negotiations.



## 31 July 1974 — Local advances after Geneva

Scheduled: 31 July 1974.

Source references: S/11353/Add.12, paragraphs 1–2 (31 July); S/11353/Add.13, paragraph 2 (1 August; southern Karavas entered, outside Lapithos)

Renewed fighting west of Girne is followed by the National Guard's withdrawal from Alsancak/Karavas and Lapta/Lapithos. Withdrawal does not by itself establish that Turkish troops have occupied both towns completely.

Turkish forces advance approximately one kilometre west from Ayios Ermolaos/Şirinevler, and approximately five hundred metres south near the Lefkoşa racecourse. These are local adjustments to the frontline, rather than a general breakout across northern Cyprus.



## 1 August 1974 — Into southern Alsancak

Scheduled: 1 August 1974.

Source references: S/11353/Add.13, paragraphs 1–3 (1–2 August); S/11353/Add.7, paragraph 3 (UN-held airport)

Turkish troops enter the southern part of Alsancak/Karavas, but remain outside Lapta/Lapithos. Firing and shelling continue to the west and southwest of Girne.

In the evening, Turkish forces enter Bellapais. This is the entry recorded in the UN's account of 1 August; earlier reports had already described Greek Cypriots there under UN protection. The airport in Lefkoşa remains outside Turkish control.



## 2–13 August 1974 — The uneasy ceasefire

Scheduled: 13 August 1974.

Source references: S/11353/Add.13–15 (2–5 August); S/11353/Add.16–18 (6–9 August); Varnava and Others v. Turkey, accounts of Turkish entry into Lapithos on 6 August; S/11353/Add.20, paragraphs 1–3 (10–12 August); S/11568, paragraph 6 (period through 13 August)

The ceasefire has reduced fighting across much of the island, but has not frozen every local position. In the Alsancak–Lapta area, Turkish troops move their forward line approximately three hundred metres west on 4 August. A larger assault follows on 6 August, with Turkish troops entering Lapta and fighting continuing around both towns into 7 August.

Along the Lefkoşa Green Line, Turkish Cypriot fighters move into houses beyond the line and erect roadblocks on 7 August. Later exchanges of fire occur around the Kythrea forest, the northeastern edge of the enclave and the Lefkoşa–Larnaca district boundary. These incidents do not establish the capture of Değirmenlik/Kythrea.

On 11–12 August, the National Guard withdraws from several Turkish Cypriot villages and town sectors in the south and west. UNFICYP assumes their protection; these withdrawals are not advances by the Turkish Army. Apart from the Alsancak–Lapta operation and small movements within Lefkoşa, the UN's retrospective account records no broad new expansion before 14 August. Negotiations continue while the island remains divided by an uneasy ceasefire.


## Score and reward rules
Fresh campaigns: Failure 0–359, Successful 360–539, Massive 540–720. Rewards remain +0/+2/+4 and are paid once when the July 30 ending appears. Later rolls do not modify the awarded ending or its leverage reward. Older campaigns which missed dated events use proportional thresholds based on the decisions actually recorded; previously paid leverage is preserved and only a higher final reward adds the difference. July 30 resolution stays on that date for the ending; Continue then advances to July 31. Other scored events advance one day on selection.
